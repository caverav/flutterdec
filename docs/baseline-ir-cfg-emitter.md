# Pipeline phase baseline: A/A noise floor at 1371e42

Two independent A/A runs of the phase harness, measured before any product
change, to establish what the ruler reports when nothing has changed. Both sides
of both runs are the same product revision built to the same bytes, so every
non-zero delta below is measurement error by construction.

This document replaces the baseline recorded at harness `b4b1d8c` in commit
`3aa2fe4`. That run and the one before it are void, for reasons and with
artifacts listed in [baseline/invalidated-runs.md](baseline/invalidated-runs.md).
Nothing from them is used here.

Everything below is reproducible from the artifacts in `docs/baseline/`. The
harness patch is committed alongside its digest; applying it to a worktree at
`1371e42` reconstructs both binaries.

artifacts alone.

## 5. Schedule and pair order

Three one-time unmeasured warmup passes per binary, run once before any measured
pair. Zero warmups inside every measured pair. Fifteen interleaved pairs.

Execution order alternates inside the pair: reference first on even pair
indexes, candidate first on odd ones. The order that actually executed is
appended by the measuring function itself, not by the loop, and is recorded per
run in `docs/baseline/aa-*/pair-order.tsv`. The harness restates the schedule
independently after the loop and diffs it against that log; both runs printed
`pair order verified alternating across 15 pairs`.

The audit in section 9 restates the schedule a third time, from outside the
harness, and confirms all 30 rows of both logs. It also confirms every measured
run document carries its own pair index and position in its label, which is what
ties a sample to the slot it was measured in. Both runs produce the same 30-row
log, so the two `pair-order.tsv` files are byte-identical
(`cb6d84f1525e1f22bbb22d43d7eb14271b246fadbd45a9883bdc7406784de98f`); so are the
two `binding.txt` files, which carry no run-identifying field.

Fifteen is odd, so the split is 8 pairs reference-first and 7 pairs
candidate-first. That residual one-pair imbalance is inherent to the
contract-mandated 15 pairs and is quantified in section 8.

## 6. Correctness

The correctness pass runs once per binary in the warmup invocation and covers all
33 disclosed cases: block count, successors, dense ids, predecessor reciprocity,
non-empty artifact, function declaration, no dangling helper call, and emission
determinism.

All four binaries: 33 of 33 cases passed, `correctness_failures` empty. See
`docs/baseline/aa-*/warmup-{reference,candidate}.json`.

Every case's `workload_sha256` is identical across all four binaries, and the two
sides' workload manifests are byte-identical in both runs (the harness diffs them
and refuses to measure otherwise).

The large per-phase MADs on the nanosecond medians are the spread across the 33
cases, which span 64 to 1024 blocks; they are not run-to-run variance. The paired
delta and its noise are what characterise the ruler.

Per case and phase, all 330 cells across both runs, with medians, median absolute
deviations, deltas, noise and MDE: `docs/baseline/per-case-summary.tsv`. Raw
per-sample streams: `docs/baseline/aa-*/samples-{reference,candidate}.tsv`, 15
pairs by 33 cases by 5 phases per side per run, 2475 rows each. Zero unpaired
samples in either run.

No cell in either run clears its own MDE. Largest absolute cell
delta is 0.0314 in aa-1 (`diamond-chain/64/heavy` serialization, MDE 0.0683) and
0.0766 in aa-2 (`fan-in/64/base` ir, MDE 0.2502). The aa-2 outlier is the
smallest and noisiest case in the matrix; its own noise MAD of 0.083 puts its MDE
far above it.

Run to run, comparing the same 165 cells between aa-1 and aa-2: median shift
-0.00031, MAD 0.00624, so the run-to-run MDE is 0.0500, the floor. The two
independent runs agree.

## 8. Order bias and sign balance

Measured directly rather than assumed. Within one binary, comparing its
first-position pairs against its second-position pairs on the combined span:

| Run | Side | (second - first) / first | MAD | firsts | seconds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| aa-1 | reference | -0.00154 | 0.00421 | 8 | 7 |
| aa-1 | candidate | +0.00309 | 0.00247 | 7 | 8 |
| aa-2 | reference | -0.00461 | 0.00399 | 8 | 7 |
| aa-2 | candidate | -0.00184 | 0.00600 | 7 | 8 |

The position effect no longer has a consistent sign: it is -0.15 to -0.46 percent
for reference and +0.31 to -0.18 percent for candidate, so it does not survive as
a schedule artefact after alternation.

Holding position fixed and comparing the two binaries only against each other in
the same slot, which is where the previous harness showed a build-layout bias at
full strength:

| Run | Phase | candidate vs reference, both first | both second |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| aa-1 | emission_exclusive | -0.00190 | +0.00312 |
| aa-1 | combined | +0.00033 | +0.00395 |
| aa-2 | emission_exclusive | -0.00422 | -0.00296 |
| aa-2 | combined | -0.00431 | -0.00050 |

At harness `b4b1d8c` these four cells read +0.011, +0.007, +0.016, +0.010 with
both slots agreeing in sign and magnitude, which is the signature of two
different binaries. Here the magnitudes are three to five times smaller and the
sign flips between slots within aa-1 and between runs on `emission_exclusive`
(+0.00065 in aa-1, -0.00309 in aa-2). Two sides built to one digest cannot carry
a build-layout bias, and the residual behaves like noise rather than a fixed
offset.

Sign balance is now centred, which it was not before:

| Run | cells | candidate-slower | candidate-faster | \|delta\| >= 2% | of those slower |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| aa-1 | 165 | 86 | 79 | 8 | 3 |
| aa-2 | 165 | 83 | 82 | 5 | 5 |

At `b4b1d8c` the same tables read 116 and 109 candidate-slower, with 20 of 28
two-percent cells one-way in aa-2. The 5 to 8 remaining two-percent cells per run
are split across both directions and none clears its own MDE.

The published noise floor is therefore the 5 percent MDE floor on
`emission_exclusive` and `combined` in both runs, and on `ir` in aa-2. Three
times the noise wins on `serialization` in both runs (0.0679 and 0.0790), on `ir`
and `cfg` in aa-1 (0.0539 and 0.0518), and on `cfg` in aa-2 (0.0506).

## 9. Independent audit

`docs/baseline/audit-aa-runs.py` recomputes every number above from the raw
sample streams, restates the alternating schedule from scratch, and exits 1 on
any of: the two runs overlapping in time, a missing or duplicated sample, a
sample key present on one side only, a binary digest mismatch between sides, a
matrix or per-case workload digest that differs between binaries, a manifest that
differs between sides or runs, a correctness failure, a memory or timeout limit
breach, a reconciliation failure, a measured pass whose four spans exceed or fail
to reconcile with its combined span, a warmup document that claims a non-zero
worst unaccounted fraction it never measured, a warmup or run count other than 3
and 0 for warmups and 0 and 1 for measured passes, a measured document whose
binding does not match `binding.txt`, a label that does not carry its pair and
position, a
cell clearing its own MDE in an A/A run, a position effect reaching the 5 percent
floor, or a held-position A/A skew of the same sign in both slots (the
build-layout signature).

Run against the live output directories, all checks active:

```
python3 docs/baseline/audit-aa-runs.py /tmp/bench-aa-1 /tmp/bench-aa-2
```

exit 0, `no reject condition triggered`. Output:
`docs/baseline/audit-live-runs.txt`.

Run against the committed artifacts alone, which regenerates the per-case
summary:

```
python3 docs/baseline/audit-aa-runs.py \
  docs/baseline/aa-1 docs/baseline/aa-2 docs/baseline/per-case-summary.tsv
```

exit 0, same numbers, output `docs/baseline/audit-committed-runs.txt`. Three
checks are skipped there and say so on stdout rather than passing silently: the
overlap check, because a committed copy carries checkout modification times and
not measurement ones, the per-pair document binding check, because the committed
evidence keeps the aggregated sample streams instead of the 60 raw per-pair
documents, and the side-versus-side manifest check, because the committed
evidence keeps only the reference manifest. All three are exercised by the live
run above. Nothing else degrades: in particular the section 4 reconciliation
numbers and every statistic in sections 7 and 8 are recomputed in full from the
committed streams, so a reader with only this repository can reproduce them after
the live directories are gone.

The reconciliation check has provable detection power. Against a copy of the
committed artifacts with one `emission_exclusive` sample tripled, so the four
parts exceed their combined span, the audit exits 1 with
`worst=-1.416358 ... negative=1` and rejects. With one `serialization` sample
zeroed instead, so the residue stays positive but grows, it exits 1 with
`worst=+0.237064` over the 0.02 tolerance. With a warmup document edited to claim
`worst_unaccounted_fraction` 0.0004 it exits 1 naming that document.

The audit's per-phase deltas and noise agree with the harness aggregator's own
`analysis.json` to within 1e-4 on all 10 phase rows, so the two implementations
of the statistic are independent and consistent.

## 11. Limits

- Fifteen pairs is odd, so the alternating schedule cannot split positions
  evenly. Residual position exposure is one pair in fifteen.
- The residual A/A skew is now noise-shaped rather than a fixed offset, but it is
  not zero: `emission_exclusive` reads +0.07 percent in one run and -0.31 percent
  in the other. A product change worth less than a few tenths of a percent on one
  phase is not separable in a single run of this size, well inside the 5 percent
  MDE floor.
- The `cfg` span shares an allocation counter with emission and is not reported
  separately, because reading the counter inside the CFG span is the one place the
  instrumentation deliberately does not reach.
- Both runs are single-host and single-session, on an otherwise idle machine.
  Nothing here characterises cross-machine variance, and nothing here bounds what
  a loaded machine would report.
- These are A/A runs against the disclosed matrix only. The held-out matrix is
  not exercised, and by contract its seed is drawn after the candidate commit by
  an independent validator.
- The identity gate compares whole-binary digests. It proves the two sides are or
  are not the same machine code; it does not localise a difference to a crate or
  a function.

## 12. Reproducing

```
git worktree add --detach <TREE> 1371e42
git -C <TREE> apply --whitespace=nowarn docs/baseline/harness-8e7f080.patch
```

then the section 2 command. The harness refuses to reuse an output directory that
already holds raw samples, holds an exclusive lock on both the output directory
and the canonical build path for the whole run, and exits non-zero if the two
sides' workload manifests differ, if the executed pair order is not the
alternating schedule, or if the identity gate rejects the pair of binaries.
